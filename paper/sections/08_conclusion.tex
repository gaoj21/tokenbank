\section{Conclusion}

A plan--execute--review team often costs several times a single agent, some tasks it solves do not
need it, and it sometimes misses answers a single agent gets right. Whether to pay for it is a constrained decision with a signed loss, solved by a confidence threshold when escalation helps less the
more confident the cheap answer is. \climb{} certifies that family with a variance-adaptive betting test whose bound holds, given i.i.d.\ data, whether or not the condition does. Uncertified rules of similar average cost can lose up to 16 points, while \climb{}'s mean loss stayed within the tolerance on every setting, saving 38\% of the pipeline's tokens on average at 5 points and 65\% at 10. Where confidence misleads, it saves little rather than losing accuracy.
