\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Scope of the guarantee.} As with other distribution-free risk-control methods, the
guarantee assumes that calibration and deployment tasks, and the model's outputs on them, share one
distribution, so thresholds are best calibrated on the target tasks and serving stack
(Appendix~\ref{app:sens}). Once records exist, recalibration takes under a second (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}), and weighted
or online calibration could extend the guarantee to shifting distributions.

\paragraph{Calibration data.} \climb{} needs every rung's outcome on a few hundred calibration tasks, a
one-off cost that is typically repaid within a few thousand queries (\S\ref{sec:tradeoff}). With less data or
tighter tolerances it falls back to the full pipeline, trading savings for the guarantee. Stronger
confidence signals could plug in directly and raise the savings.
